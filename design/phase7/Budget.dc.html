Building Awhat the renderer can do, and what was decided
A GDI+ layered window canDraw true arcs with antialiased edges and per-pixel alpha, so there is no rectangular window edge and the shadow is part of the bitmap. Hit testing is angle and radius arithmetic, so there are no dead gaps between wedges. Repainting the whole 320px bitmap on every pointer move costs well under a millisecond.
It cannotHold native controls, so no scrolling list and no text field inside the ring. Text is drawn by GDI+, which means grayscale antialiasing rather than the ClearType the card uses: fine at 11px and up, marginally softer. Every hover repaints the whole bitmap.
Both gestures, one ringHold the trigger and release over a wedge, or let go and click. The hub is the dead zone for both: release or click there and nothing happens. A minimum travel of about 30px stops a twitch from picking.
Keyboard survivesEvery wedge keeps its letter, and the letters are the ones already assigned, so the list card and the ring stay interchangeable.
DecidedThe ring replaces the list: Z always opens it, and the letters still pick while it is open. It opens at the pointer, clamped to the monitor. The paste card dims to half behind it. Action mode opens its ring from Ctrl+Shift+A only. Wedges are 8px apart with 8px corners and show one short word each; the full name and a description appear in the hub on hover.
Rough costOne renderer of about 300 lines (geometry, paint, hit test, input), shared by both rings, plus the two call sites. The list menus go; the help card and confirm prompts stay on the card.
