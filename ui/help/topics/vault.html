The Vault

Everything else in Clipjump expires. Clips fall off the end of the stack as you copy, and Clipboard History is pruned by age and by count. The Vault is the exception: nothing removes an entry from it but you. It is where a signature, a template, a licence key or an address belongs, rather than hoping it is still somewhere in your history next month.

Putting something in

Three ways, whichever suits where you are:

  • In Clipboard History, right-click a row and choose Save to Vault.
  • In the Channel Organizer, the same entry sits on a clip's menu.
  • In the Vault itself, press Add from clipboard to save whatever you just copied.

New entries land at the end of the list. Nothing is ever merged: if you save the same text twice you get two entries, because you may have put them there for different reasons.

Keeping it tidy

Switch between the two lists with the History and Vault buttons at the top of the Clipboard History window.

An entry can be given a short name, which is what the lists show in place of its text. That is worth doing for anything you reach for often, because a name is far easier to recognise at a glance than the first line of a paragraph.

You set the order yourself with Alt+Up and Alt+Down, or from the right-click menu. The column headers do not sort here, and they deliberately show no sort arrow: the Vault has one order, yours, and it is the same order paste mode walks. Put the entries you use daily at the top.

Ctrl+H edits an entry's text, and its kind is worked out again from whatever you type, so a note that becomes a link starts behaving like a link. Del asks before it removes an entry, because the Vault is the only copy.

Pasting from it

Hold Ctrl, tap V as usual, then press B. The card says Vault, and V and C now walk your saved entries instead of your recent clips, wrapping round at either end. Release Ctrl to paste the one you are on. Press B again to go back to the clip stack exactly where you left it.

Keys that act on a clip's position do nothing while you are in the Vault: fixing, tagging, the number jumps, the X mode cycle and the channel keys. The card's key chips show the short list that does apply.

Two things worth knowing

The Vault is not encrypted. Entries sit in the same database as your history, readable by anything that can read the folder. The name is about permanence, not secrecy, so it is not the place for a password.

An image saved from a Clipboard History row is that row's stored copy, which is a JPEG and has lost any transparency: by the time a clip reaches history the original pixels are gone. Saved straight after copying, or from the Channel Organizer, you get the original.
