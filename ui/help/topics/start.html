Start here

Clipjump keeps everything you copy. Each copy goes on top of a stack, and paste mode walks down that stack instead of pasting only the newest clip.

Paste mode

Hold Ctrl and press V. Keep holding Ctrl: each further press of V moves forward one clip and C moves back one. The card near your pointer shows which clip you are on. Release Ctrl to paste it.

Clipjump counts from the newest clip, so moving forward, or ahead, means going further down the stack into what you copied earlier, and moving back means returning toward the newest. The card and the key lists use those words throughout.

Channels

Clips live in channels, which are separate stacks. Up and Down in paste mode switch channels, and the Channel Organizer moves clips between them.

The pit

The pit is not a special feature: it is an ordinary channel that happens to be named pit, and it does not exist until you make it. Create a channel with that name in the Channel Organizer and one thing changes: anything you copy while the pit is the active channel is kept out of Clipboard History. The clip still goes on the pit's own stack, so you can paste it as usual, but no record of it is written to the history database.

That makes it the place for copying you would rather not leave behind, a password on its way into a form, or a burst of throwaway clips you do not want cluttering your history. The pit-swap shortcut on the Shortcuts page jumps to it and back to whichever channel you were in, so it is one key in and one key out. Without a channel named pit, that key just tells you it could not find one.

It is the opposite end of the scale from the Vault, which has its own topic: the pit is for clips you want no trace of, the Vault is for clips you want to keep for good.

The rings

Two things open a ring at your pointer: Z in paste mode opens the format ring, and Action Mode opens the action ring. Up to eight wedges sit around a hub that names whichever wedge you point at. When there are more entries than that, the eighth wedge is More and turns the page.

Flick out to a wedge and release the key you are holding, click a wedge, or press its letter; Esc closes the ring.
